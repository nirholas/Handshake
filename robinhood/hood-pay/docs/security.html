Security model

hood-pay is non-custodial by construction. It moves no money on its own and stores no keys. Here is exactly what each piece can and cannot do.

The widget CAN

  • Discover EIP-6963 wallets already installed in the buyer's browser.
  • Ask the wallet to switch to Robinhood Chain (4663 / 46630).
  • Read the buyer's token balance and allowance (read-only eth_call).
  • Build an unsigned transfer (or approve + router pay) and hand it to the wallet to sign.
  • Watch for the transaction receipt and show a confirmation state.

The widget CANNOT

  • Hold, request, or see a private key or seed phrase - ever.
  • Custody funds. Payments go buyer → merchant (or buyer → router → merchant) in one atomic transaction; the router never holds a balance.
  • Move funds the buyer did not just approve for that exact call.
  • Decide, at settlement, that a payment "counts" from the DOM. The merchant's verifier re-reads the amount on-chain.
  • Approve unlimited allowance - router mode approves the exact invoice amount.

Amounts are verified on-chain, never in the DOM

A malicious page could change what a buyer sees, but it cannot forge a settled transfer. The merchant side (hood-pay/verify) reads Transfer / PaymentReceived logs directly from the chain and sums the attributed value in BigInt. The webhook body and the widget's onSuccess receipt are hints; the ledger and watcher are the source of truth.

Reorg-depth policy

Robinhood Chain is an Arbitrum Orbit L2 with ~100 ms blocks. The watcher defends against reorgs two ways:

  • Confirmation depth. Only blocks at depth ≥ confirmations below the head are ever scanned (default 30, roughly 3 seconds of blocks). A log must survive that many blocks before it can count.
  • Canonical re-check. Every candidate log's block hash is re-fetched by number and compared. If the block was reorged out after the scan, the hash mismatches and the log is dropped.

Together, a confirmed result is stable under any reorg shallower than confirmations, and immune to deeper ones that occur between scanning and re-checking. Raise confirmations for higher-value invoices.

Reference scheme + collision math

USDG is a plain ERC-20 whose transfer carries no memo or invoice-reference field, so a bare transfer cannot say which invoice it settles. hood-pay attributes payments two ways. (Note: current mainnet USDG also exposes an EIP-2612 permit, but hood-pay does not use it - it approves the exact amount explicitly, so there is one auditable allowance path.)

Router mode (exact attribution). The buyer pays through the stateless HoodPayRouter, which emits PaymentReceived(reference, payer, payTo, token, amount). The reference is 32 random bytes per checkout, drawn uniformly from 2256. By the birthday bound, the probability any two of k references collide is about k² / 2²⁵⁷ - at a trillion payments (k = 10¹²) that is ~8.6 × 10⁻⁵⁴. Attribution is exact regardless of amount, so partial payments and overpayments are summed per reference.

Direct mode (amount fingerprinting, zero contracts). USDG has 6 decimals; retail prices use at most 2, leaving the low 4 decimal digits (9,999 usable slots, each worth 0.0001 USDG) as a fingerprint. The verifier matches Transfer(to = payTo, value = base + dust) exactly.

  • With a ledger, the dust slot is atomically reserved per (payTo, token, base amount) while an invoice is open - collisions are impossible by construction, not by probability.
  • Without a ledger (static payment links), dust is random and the birthday bound applies: k(k-1) / (2 × 9999) for k concurrent open invoices at the same price to the same merchant - about 0.45% at k = 10. Static links suit low-concurrency sellers; a ledger or router suits everyone else.

Merchant key handling

Refunds are the only operation that needs the merchant's key, and hood-pay never touches it. buildRefundTx produces an unsigned transfer; sendRefund drives a wallet client the merchant supplies. Keep the key in ROBINHOOD_CHAIN_PRIVATE_KEY (or a signer service), never in code, and never in a client bundle.

Stock Tokens are out of scope, on purpose

hood-pay settles in USDG and other non-security tokens. It does not buy, sell, or route Robinhood Stock Tokens. Stock Tokens are tokenized debt securities (issuer: Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Ltd) and may not be offered, sold, or delivered to US persons (with extra limits in Canada, the UK, and Switzerland). If you point a checkout at a Stock Token as the payment asset, that eligibility restriction is yours to enforce; USDG and memecoin payments carry no such restriction.
