What ERC-8056 is

ERC-8056 ("Scaled UI Amount Extension for ERC-20 Tokens", draft, created 2025-10-20, co-authored by engineers from Superstate and Robinhood) adds one updatable number to an ERC-20: a display multiplier. 

function uiMultiplier() external view returns (uint256); // 18-decimal fixed point, 1e18 = 1.0

Raw balances never move. balanceOf and totalSupply are untouched by corporate actions. When a company splits its stock 2-for-1, the issuer does not mint tokens to thousands of holders; it sets the multiplier to 2e18 and emits one event. Every interface that respects the standard then displays doubled positions, because the true position is: 

trueBalance = balanceOf(account) * uiMultiplier() / 1e18

The multiplier is a cosmetic layer with economic meaning: it is how many underlying units (shares, for tokenized equities) one raw token represents. It starts at 1e18 and moves on splits, reverse splits, and reinvested distributions. The same pattern exists on Solana as the Token-2022 scaled-UI-amount extension; ERC-8056 is its EVM counterpart.

Why it exists

Without it, an issuer handling a 2-for-1 split has two bad options:

  • Mint to every holder. Gas-intensive, operationally risky, and it breaks accounting in every DeFi protocol holding the token (pool reserves, collateral records, vault shares all go stale at once).
  • Rebase. Rewriting balances has the same DeFi-breaking problem, famously so: rebasing tokens are widely unsupported by AMMs and lending markets.

ERC-8056 takes a third path: raw amounts are stable forever; display scales. DeFi keeps working because the token it integrates never changes shape. The cost is moved to the read side: every UI, indexer, and valuation engine must apply the multiplier, exactly once, in exactly the right place. That is the entire failure mode of the standard, and the reason this page and the erc8056 package exist.

A real instance, from SGOV's reinvested dividend: block 4629631 carries UIMultiplierUpdated(1000000000000000000, 1000957519890990718, 1783541672): from 1.0 to 1.000957…, effective 2026-07-08 20:14:32 UTC.

Scheduled changes

Corporate actions are announced before they happen, and the standard mirrors that: newUIMultiplier() and effectiveAt() expose the scheduled change, and uiMultiplier() flips to the new value once the timestamp passes. One observed subtlety of the deployed implementation: after activation, newUIMultiplier() and effectiveAt() keep the last applied values rather than resetting. "A change is pending" therefore means effectiveAt() > block.timestamp && newUIMultiplier() != uiMultiplier(), not merely effectiveAt() != 0.

The two rules (with real on-chain numbers)

Everything an integrator must get right reduces to two rules. The numbers below are not hypothetical; they were read from Robinhood Chain mainnet at block 10,745,112 (2026-07-15) and are pinned by this project's test suites.

Rule 1: positions. Raw balances understate holdings.

WEEK (Roundhill Weekly T-Bill ETF token) reinvests weekly distributions. Its multiplier was 2006182524271844660: one raw WEEK token was 2.0062 underlying shares.

Wrong: show balanceOf

1.0000 WEEK
A naive tracker reads balanceOf = 1e18 and reports one share. The position is understated by 50.1%.

Right: apply the multiplier

2.0062 shares
1e18 × 2006182524271844660 / 1e18. Cross-checkable on-chain: balanceOfUI() computes exactly this, floored.

Rule 2: valuation. Know what your price is a price of.

SGOV's multiplier was 1000957519890990718 and its Chainlink feed answered 100.62147097 USD. That answer is the price of one token; the multiplier is already inside it. Value a 1-token position:

Wrong: re-apply the multiplier

$100.7178
100.62147097 × 1.000957… double-counts the dividend. Every split and distribution inflates the error further.

Right: tokens × feed price

$100.6215
balanceOf × feedAnswer / 1e18. The multiplier appears nowhere. That absence is the correctness.

The invariant: the multiplier is applied exactly once across the balance-and-price pair. Feed prices per token: apply it zero more times. Share prices from an off-chain equity quote: apply it exactly once, to the balance. The erc8056 package enforces this with branded price types; a bare number does not compile.

The price-feed question, answered definitively

"Is the Chainlink answer pre- or post-multiplier?" is the ambiguity that produces most real-world bugs, because for most tokens (multiplier still 1.0) both readings give the same number and the bug ships silently. Three independent lines of evidence settle it:

  1. The issuer says so. Robinhood's developer documentation: "The Chainlink price already includes the corporate-action multiplier (dividends, splits), so the value you read is the token's full price - don't apply the multiplier yourself."
  2. The market agrees. At block 10,745,112 the Uniswap v3 SGOV/USDG pool (0xFfb3…c4f1, 0.01% tier) priced the token at $100.6075. The feed said $100.6215: 0.014% apart. The unadjusted share price, $100.5252 (feed ÷ multiplier), is 0.096% away. Live DEX liquidity tracks the adjusted number.
  3. The magnitudes reconcile. feed ≈ share price × multiplier holds on SGOV to within normal market noise; the relationship is not an accident of one reading, and it is asserted continuously by this project's fork tests.

Scope note: this is verified for Robinhood Chain's Stock Token feeds. The ERC-8056 spec itself does not standardize oracle behavior. If you integrate a different ERC-8056 asset, verify its oracle's convention the same way (compare against a DEX pool or the issuer's docs) before choosing which valuation branch to use.

As deployed on Robinhood Chain (chain ID 4663)

Every canonical Stock Token is a BeaconProxy resolving through the shared beacon 0xe10b6f6B275de231345c20D14Ab812db62151b00 to the verified Stock implementation (0xb354…C5aE2). Reading its verified source and ABI surfaces four facts the spec draft alone will not tell you:

  • The transfer event is named differently. The draft's optional event is TransferWithUIAmount; the deployed contract emits TransferWithScaledUI(address indexed from, address indexed to, uint256 value, uint256 uiValue), topic0 0x37e7f0db430edc9dd31bc66f25f8449353aa0818f503b906747dd8f286cd3802. Events do not affect ERC-165 IDs, so both are conformant, but an indexer subscribed to the draft's topic sees nothing.
  • The conversion extension is absent. toUIAmount/fromUIAmount are not in the ABI, calls revert, and supportsInterface(0x57854fc3) is false. Compute conversions locally.
  • Updates go through updateMultiplier. The issuer-side writers are updateMultiplier(uint256) and updateMultiplier(uint256,uint256) (value + effective timestamp), not the reference implementation's setUIMultiplier. They are access-controlled; only the issuer moves the multiplier.
  • balanceOfUI/totalSupplyUI floor. totalSupplyUI() == totalSupply() * uiMultiplier() / 1e18 holds bit-for-bit on live tokens (verified on SGOV and WEEK), so local math can replicate the chain exactly.

Integration recipes

JavaScript / TypeScript

npm install erc8056 viem - a <3 kB package whose types make the wrong valuation branch a compile error: 

import { createPublicClient, http } from 'viem'
import { robinhood } from 'viem/chains'
import {
  readUiMultiplier, readMultiplierState, watchMultiplier,
  trueBalance, trueValue, adjustedPrice, rawPrice, supportsErc8056,
} from 'erc8056'

const client = createPublicClient({ chain: robinhood, transport: http() })
const WEEK = '0xc93a8c440CEa26D7445dF01729f193b27965099f'

// Rule 1: positions
const multiplier = await readUiMultiplier(client, WEEK)   // 2006182524271844660n
const shares = trueBalance({ raw: rawBalance, multiplier }) // == on-chain balanceOfUI

// Rule 2: valuation. A bare number is not a price; declare what you hold.
trueValue({ raw, multiplier, price: adjustedPrice(feedUsd) }) // Chainlink on chain 4663
trueValue({ raw, multiplier, price: rawPrice(shareUsd) })     // off-chain share quote
// trueValue({ raw, multiplier, price: feedUsd })  <- does not compile

// Corporate-action monitoring
const unwatch = watchMultiplier(client, WEEK, (u) =>
  console.log(`multiplier ${u.oldMultiplier} -> ${u.newMultiplier} at ${u.effectiveAt}`))

// Scheduled-but-not-yet-effective changes
const { pending } = await readMultiplierState(client, WEEK)

// Non-implementers detected gracefully, no throw
await supportsErc8056(client, someToken) // false for a plain ERC-20

Solidity

The repo ships IERC8056.sol (all four interfaces + interface-ID constants) and ERC8056Consumer.sol, a commented reference consumer proven by fork tests against live tokens. The essence: 

import {IScaledUIAmount, ERC8056InterfaceIds} from "erc8056/contracts/src/IERC8056.sol";

// Detect (ERC-165 is mandatory for compliant tokens)
bool ok = IERC165(token).supportsInterface(ERC8056InterfaceIds.SCALED_UI_AMOUNT);

// Rule 1: true position
uint256 shares = IERC20(token).balanceOf(user) * IScaledUIAmount(token).uiMultiplier() / 1e18;

// Rule 2: value with a Robinhood Chain Chainlink feed - NO multiplier here,
// the feed already applied it:
(, int256 answer,,,) = feed.latestRoundData();
uint256 usdValue = IERC20(token).balanceOf(user) * uint256(answer) / 1e18;

FAQ

Is an ERC-8056 token a rebase token?

No. Rebase tokens rewrite balanceOf; ERC-8056 never does. Raw balances and totalSupply are permanent, which is why AMMs and lending markets keep working through corporate actions. Only the display layer (and your accounting of "how many shares is this") scales.

Do I multiply the Chainlink price by uiMultiplier?

On Robinhood Chain: no, never. The feed answer is already the token's full, multiplier-adjusted price (issuer-documented and market-verified above). Multiplying again double-counts every split and dividend. For other chains or issuers, verify the oracle's convention first; the spec does not standardize it.

How do I detect whether a token implements ERC-8056?

ERC-165 is mandatory: supportsInterface(0xa60bf13d). As a fallback for non-compliant partial implementers, staticcall uiMultiplier() and check for a 32-byte uint. A token with neither is a plain ERC-20, which behaves exactly like a multiplier of 1e18.

Can the multiplier go below 1e18?

Yes: a reverse split scales it down (a 1-for-2 reverse split halves it). The math is unchanged. Nothing observed on Robinhood Chain has gone below 1e18 yet; SGOV and WEEK have only moved up, via reinvested distributions.

What precision and rounding does the standard use?

The multiplier is 18-decimal fixed point (1e18 = 1.0). The deployed implementation floors: balanceOfUI = balanceOf * uiMultiplier / 1e18 with EVM integer division. The erc8056 package reproduces this bit-for-bit (asserted against live totalSupplyUI in its tests), so client-side and on-chain numbers never diverge.

Which event should my indexer subscribe to?

For corporate actions: UIMultiplierUpdated(uint256,uint256,uint256), topic0 0x2205df45…b055, emitted on every change (spec MUST). For UI-amount transfers on Robinhood Chain: TransferWithScaledUI, topic0 0x37e7f0db…3802. The draft's TransferWithUIAmount name is not what the deployed contracts emit.

Does uiMultiplier() flip automatically at effectiveAt?

Yes. The scheduled value in newUIMultiplier() becomes the answer of uiMultiplier() once block.timestamp >= effectiveAt(), with no further transaction. After activation the deployed implementation keeps the last applied values in newUIMultiplier()/effectiveAt(), so test for pending as future timestamp and differing value, not merely a nonzero timestamp.

Why does toUIAmount() revert on Stock Tokens?

Robinhood's implementation omits the optional conversion extension (supportsInterface(0x57854fc3) is false). The conversion is one multiplication; do it locally (toUiAmount/fromUiAmount in the package) and save the RPC round-trip.
