The Three.ws User Group's First In-World Meetup: How It Went

Yesterday, the Three.ws User Group on IBM Community held its first meetup, and it ran for nine hours, 8 AM to 5 PM Pacific, entirely inside three.ws itself. Not a webinar link. Not a call with a slide deck. An actual persistent 3D world, open the whole day, in the $THREE home town at three.ws/play. This is the recap from our side of it.

Why we did it this way

The group's own organizer put the argument best in the post that announced this: it is not a video call, you attend as an avatar, you hear the people standing near you through spatial voice chat, and the product tour happens by literally walking through the product. Three.ws is the only project we know of with its own dedicated group on IBM Community, and that only made sense to us if the group's first meetup actually used what the group exists to talk about, instead of describing it from a slide.

What the day looked like

The formal program ran the way it was scheduled: a live tour of the platform from inside the world, community demos from people who brought what they had built, and open Q&A with the team, including real questions about the two IBM-native pieces of the stack, @three-ws/ibm-watsonx-mcp (watsonx.ai on your own IBM Cloud account) and @three-ws/ibm-x402-mcp (pay-per-use IBM Granite billed in USDC, no IBM account required).

Around that program, the day looked less like a corporate meetup and more like people actually spending time somewhere together: fishing at the world's crafting stations, cooking what got caught, driving vehicles around the map, and building on the shared voxel layer in the plaza. None of that was scripted. It is the same in-game economy and building layer every /play world runs on: vendor NPCs, banking, quests, vehicles, and persistent collaborative building, used for nine hours by people who came for a user group meetup and stayed because the venue turned out to be a real place.

What it proved, beyond the vibe

Underneath the fun, a nine-hour, all-day meetup inside a live multiplayer world is a real stress test, run in front of an audience that would notice if it did not hold up: an authoritative Colyseus server on Cloud Run, 15 Hz binary delta sync, and proximity-gated spatial voice, carrying real conversations for the length of a full workday. That is the part that matters for anyone deciding whether "meet inside the product" is a gimmick or a format worth repeating.

The other side of this recap

The group's organizer wrote her own account of the day, live during the event and again once it wrapped, in her own voice, on the Three.ws User Group. Read it on the group's recent blogs page; it is the honest, personal half of this story, and this post is not a substitute for it.

What's next

The world stays open. Anyone can walk into the $THREE home town at three.ws/play today, no download, no account, no wallet required to look around. The group stays open too, at the Three.ws User Group on IBM Community, and event infrastructure for /play, scheduled community events surfaced in the world lobby, richer creator controls, is next on the roadmap specifically because of how this one went.
