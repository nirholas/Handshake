# Thank-you reply: the Coinbase listing moment

Reply copy for **@nichxbt** under [the listing post](https://x.com/nichxbt) that
[@jessepollak](https://x.com/jessepollak) answered with "huge congrats!!!!" on 2026-08-21.

**Tone:** sincere, warm, confident. Not a joke, not a flex, not a price post. This is a
thank-you to a builder from a builder, and the only currency in it is credit.

**Verified true today:** $THREE lives on Solana at
`FeMbDoX7R1Psc4GEcvJdsbNbZA3bfztcyDCatJVJpump`; three.ws settles real x402 micropayments in
USDC on Solana and Base, on a loop that runs every minute with real on-chain settlement and no
mocks ([`docs/autonomous-x402.md`](../autonomous-x402.md)); agent identity is registered against
the ERC-8004 registries on Base mainnet (chain 8453), where a registration costs roughly
$0.10 to $0.25 in gas ([`docs/erc8004.md`](../erc8004.md), [`docs/smart-contracts.md`](../smart-contracts.md));
the partner ecosystem at [three.ws/partners](https://three.ws/partners) covers OpenAI, IBM, AWS,
Google Cloud, Alibaba Cloud, NVIDIA, Quicknode, and HackerNoon.

**Do not claim:** a negotiated or centralized Coinbase listing agreement, any Coinbase
partnership, any endorsement by Jesse Pollak or the Base team, any price or market-cap number,
or that buybacks are executing. Jesse's own post is the source: every token on Base and Solana
is now surfaced in the main Coinbase app. Thank the change, never inflate it.

**Format:** this is a long-form reply and it is well past 280 characters, so it posts from the

## The reply (post this)

> Thank you, Jesse. Truly.
>
> I have been building long enough to know what a reply like that is worth on a day like this,
> and it is worth more than the listing. You did not have to say anything. You said something
> anyway, and you said it first, and you said it with real warmth. That is the part I will
> remember when the chart has done whatever the chart is going to do.
>
> So let me say thank you properly, because a two-word reply does not cover it.
>
> Thank you for building. Not announcing, not positioning, not reserving the right to build
> later. Building. Base did not arrive as a promise, it arrived as a chain people could ship on
> the same afternoon they heard about it, with gas cheap enough that a single person with an
> idea and no funding could put something real on it. That decision has quietly paid for
> thousands of experiments that would never have survived a mainnet gas bill. Ours is one of
> them.
>
> Thank you for innovating out loud. Killing the centralized listing process is not a feature
> release, it is a statement about who the market is for. For years the honest answer to a small
> team asking "how do we get listed" was: know someone, wait, hope. Today the answer is: ship
> something worth holding and the door is already open. You turned a gate into a road. Every
> founder who is currently building in a bedroom at 2am with no contacts and no budget just got
> handed the same distribution as everyone else, and most of them do not even know it yet. They
> will.
>
> Thank you for x402. This one is personal for us. three.ws is not a token with an app attached,
> it is an economy of AI agents that pay each other, and payment was the hard part for years.
> Agents could think, they could call tools, they could hold a wallet, but they could not
> transact in the one motion the web already understands: a request, a 402, a signature, a
> settlement, a response. x402 gave that back to HTTP. Our agents now settle real USDC per call,
> on Solana and on Base, on a loop that runs every single minute of every single day, with
> receipts, spend caps, and no simulation anywhere in the path. When an agent on our platform
> buys market intel, it actually pays for it. When someone builds a service on us, other agents
> can actually buy it. That is not a demo. That is a working market, and the standard underneath
> it came from your side of the table.
>
> Thank you for the contracts we build on. Our agent identity lives on Base at the ERC-8004
> registries, which means an agent created on three.ws is not a row in our database, it is a
> public, verifiable, portable identity that outlives us. Anyone can resolve it. Anyone can check
> who owns it. Anyone can read its reputation without asking our permission or trusting our
> uptime. Registration costs a couple of dimes and confirms in a couple of seconds. Ten years of
> crypto infrastructure had to work for that sentence to be boring, and on Base it is boring,
> which is the highest compliment infrastructure can receive.
>
> And thank you to Coinbase. Not for the listing itself, for the philosophy behind it. Choosing
> to surface what the market chose instead of what a committee approved is a hard call to make
> when you are the biggest venue in the room, and it gives up real gatekeeping power on purpose.
> That is the kind of decision that only makes sense if you actually believe the open version
> wins. We believe it too. It is why every line of three.ws is Apache-2.0 and why our SDKs, our
> MCP servers, and our agent stack are public for anyone to fork, including our competitors.
>
> To our partners: OpenAI, IBM, AWS, Google Cloud, Alibaba Cloud, NVIDIA, Quicknode, HackerNoon.
> Thank you for taking a small team seriously before there was any reason to. Every one of you
> said yes at a stage where saying no would have been the safe, defensible, completely reasonable
> answer. You gave us compute, distribution, review, credibility, and in more than one case a
> direct line to an engineer who fixed our problem on a weekend. None of that shows up in a
> ticker. All of it shows up in the product.
>
> To the $THREE holders, and I mean the ones who were here months ago when there was nothing to
> look at but a roadmap and a promise: you funded this. Not abstractly. Literally. You held
> through quiet weeks that would have broken a less patient community, and you kept showing up in
> the channel asking good questions instead of asking when. Whatever this becomes, you were early
> in the only sense that ever mattered, which is that you were early on purpose.
>
> Here is what I want to say to anyone else reading this who is building something and wondering
> whether it is worth it.
>
> It is worth it. The infrastructure you needed five years ago exists now. Blockspace is cheap.
> Identity is standardized. Payments are a header. Distribution is no longer a phone call you are
> not connected enough to make. Every excuse that used to be legitimate has been systematically
> dismantled by people who chose to make the ladder public instead of pulling it up behind them.
> All that is left is the work, and the work has always been the fun part.
>
> We are going to keep our head down and earn this. More agents, more open source, more of the
> economy wired end to end, and every bit of it shipped in public where you can check it. The
> listing is not the finish line and it was never the goal. It is a bigger room. We intend to
> deserve it.
>
> Thank you, genuinely, from all of us at three.ws.
>
> Now back to building.
